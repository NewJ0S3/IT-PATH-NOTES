# Introduction to IT Support

## **What is IT?:**

Is defined as the use of digital technology like computers and the internet to store and process data into useful information.

## **The scope and importance of IT:**

- IT encompasses various jobs such as network engineers, hardware technicians, and desktop support personal.
- IT is people-centered, helping solve meaningful problems and enabling collaboration across industries like education, medicine and entertainment.

## **The Digital Divide and Social Impact:**

- The digital divide refers to the gap between those with digital literacy skills and access to technology and those without.
- IT professionals play a key role in bridging this divide by helping communities gain access and skills, fostering opportunities for diverse perspectives in the industry.

## **Role and responsibilities of IT Support:**

- IT support specialist ensure an organization’s technology runs smoothly by managing, installing, maintaining, troubleshooting, and configuring equipment.
- The work varies greatly depending on the environment, such as in-person vs remote support and small business vs. large enterprise.

## History of Computing

**Computer** is a device that stores and processes data by performing calculations.

### **Early Computing Devices:**

- The term “computer” originally referred to a person who performed calculations manually.
- The abacus, invented around 500 BC, is one of the earliest known computing tools and is still used today.

### **Mechanical Calculators and Programmable Machines:**

- Blaise Pascal invented the mechanical calculator in the 17th century, automating basic arithmetic operations.
- Joseph Jacquard’s programmable loom used punch cards to create fabric patterns, influencing later computing technology.

### **Foundations of Modern Computing:**

- Charles Babbage developed the difference engine and the analytical engine, early mechanical computers using punch cards.
- Ada Lovelace recognized the analytical engine’s potential beyond calculations and created the first computer algorithm, making the beginning of computer programming.

**Algorithm** is a series of steps that solve specific problems.

**Cryptography** is the art of writing and solving codes.

### **Early Computing and WWII Impact:**

- Early computers like the analytical engine were large and expensive, with limited progress until World War II.
- The war spurred government investment in computing research, advancing fields like cryptography and enabling faster processing of secret messages.

### **Technological Advancements Post-War:**

- Post-War, companies like IBM and Hewlett Packard advanced computing technology for business, academic, and government use.
- Innovations such as magnetic tape replace punch cards for data storage, and vacuum tubes were eventually replaced by more efficient transistors.

### **Rise of Personal Computing and Sofware:**

- The invention of compilers by Grace Hopper made programming more accessible, leading to the development of operating systems and smaller computers.
- The consumer computing era began with affordable personal computers like the Apple II and IBM PC, supported by operating systems such as MS-DOS and Microsoft Windows.

### **Emergence of Open Source and Mobile Computing:**

- Richard Stallman developed the free GNU operating system, laying the groundwork for Linux, a major open-source OS.
- The introduction of PDAs and smartphones in the 1990s marked the shift to mobile computing, making powerful computers portable.

### **Impact on IT Support and Future Outlook:**

- Early IT support involved hardware maintenance like replacing vacuum tubes, evolving to support complex software and networks.
- The rapid evolution of computing technology continues to expand the role and skills required for IT professionals, with future possibilities including virtual reality interfaces.

## Digital Logic

**Binary system** is the communication that a computer uses, also known as a base-2 numeral system.

### **Understanding Binary System and Computing:**

- Computers perform calculations by processing data as ones and zeros, known as the binary system or base-2 numeral system.
- The binary system allows computers to execute millions or billions of simple calculations per second, enabling complex tasks.

### **Bits, Bytes, and Computer Language:**

- Binary digits are grouped into sets of eight called bytes, which became the industry standard due to early hardware design.
- Each byte can represent one character, with 256 possible values, forming the basis of computer language and communication.

### **Practical Implications of Binary:**

- Everything displayed on a computer, including videos, images, and text, is ultimately represented as combinations of ones and zeros.
- Understanding binary is essential as it underpins all computing processes and is foundational for further learning in IT.

**Character encoding** assigns our binary values to characters, so that we as humans can read them.

**ASCII** represents the English alphabet, digits, and punctuation marks.

## Evolution of Encoding Standards

- ASCII was the earliest standard, using 127 values to represent English letters, digits, and punctuation.
- ASCII was limited, so newer standards like UTF-8 were developed to support more characters and languages.
- UTF-8 uses a variable number of bytes per character, enabling representation of emojis and many other symbols.

## Representing Color in Computers

- Colors are represented using models like RGB (red, green, blue).
- Each color channel is represented by a byte, and mixing the channels produces the full range of colors seen on screens.

**Logic gates** allow our transistors to do more complex tasks, like decide where to send electrical signals depending on logical conditions.

## Binary Representation

- Computers use binary states, represented as ones and zeros, similar to a light bulb being on (1) or off (0).
- Historically, punch cards with holes (1) or no holes (0) were used to control machines like Jacquard’s loom, demonstrating an early binary concept.

## Modern Binary Systems

- Today, computers use electrical signals through transistors to represent binary states: voltage present (1) or absent (0).
- Transistors alone are not enough; logic gates help manage electrical signals based on logical conditions to perform complex tasks.

## Programming and Binary

- Binary code allows computers to calculate numbers and execute instructions.
- Compilers translate human-readable instructions into binary code that computers can understand, enabling software like social media platforms and videogames.

## Computer Architecture Layer

**Abstraction** to take a relatively complex system and simplify it for our use

## Understanding Abstraction

- Abstraction simplifies complex systems by hiding the underlying details and providing a common interface for users.
- It allows users to interact with technology without needing to understand the technical details, such as using a mouse or keyboard instead of binary code.

## Examples of Abstraction

- Driving a car involves abstraction: drivers use steering wheels and pedals without needing to know how the engine or transmission works.
- In computing, error messages abstract complex code issues, helping users identify problems without digging into the code.

## Importance of Abstraction in IT

- Abstraction breaks down complex problems into simpler, manageable layers or concepts.
- It is a fundamental concept used throughout computing to make technology more accesible and easier to work with.

**Software layer** is how we as humans interact with our computer.

**User** is who interacts with the computer or software

## Computer System Layers

- A computer is divided into four main layers: hardware, operating system, software, and users.
- Hardware consist of physical components like laptops, phones, monitors, and keyboards.

## Operating System Layer

- The operating system enables communication between hardware and software, allowing devices from different manufactures to work together.

## Software and User Interaction

- Software is the interface through which humans interact with computers, including apps, browsers, and word processors.
- The user layer is crucial, as IT work often involves addressing user needs and problems, emphasizing the human element in technology.
